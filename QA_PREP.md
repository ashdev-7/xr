# Jury Q&A preparation

Short, honest answers. Numbers come from `results/report.md` (generated by `tools/bench/report.py` on the
final code) and `EXPERIMENTS.md`. If a number is not there, say "we did not measure that".

**1. Why not a neural network?**
The hard part is the slow, unobservable motion (bias and tilt drift at 20–60 s periods), not pattern
recognition. A network trained on ~17 public flights would learn those flights, not the physics, and it
would break the 8 MB / 20 ms budget in pure Python. Every component we kept has a physical model and a
measured gain on held-out flights. The data-driven parts are small: a handful of prior parameters learned
per platform class.

**2. Your error is metres. Is that good?**
IMU-only navigation cannot see slow motion: a 0.01 m/s² bias becomes 0.5 m after 10 s and 50 m after
100 s. The reference points are "stand still" (output a constant position) and naive double
integration (thousands of metres on the same data). On the machine hall flights, 86 % of the true motion
is in periods longer than 60 s. We beat standing still there because the rotor-drag model turns the
accelerometer into a velocity sensor.

**3. What is the rotor-drag model and why is it allowed?**
A multirotor's thrust acts along one body axis, so the specific force perpendicular to it is mostly rotor
drag, proportional to body velocity (f⊥ = −μ v⊥; Leishman et al., 2014). We verified it on ground truth
(R² up to 0.87, μ ≈ 0.18 s⁻¹, the same on every EuRoC flight) and learned μ on training flights. It uses
only the IMU; no external data.

**4. How do you know which platform it is?**
Rotor vibration. The median accelerometer noise over the first half of the flight is 0.5–1.0 m/s² on
EuRoC and ~0.03 m/s² on handheld TUM-VI: a 20× gap, larger than any jamming level in the official script.

**5. How do you measure the jamming level?**
From packet loss. The official script scales every impairment with one severity curve, including dropped
rows. Dropped rows show up as timestamp gaps that motion and vibration cannot fake, so we fit the severity
ramp to the per-second missing-row count. On drones the injected white noise is 5–12× smaller than rotor
vibration, so noise-based estimation would not work.

**6. Why hold position in the blind window for drones?**
Ground truth shows that EuRoC flights decelerate and descend at the end: speed falls from 0.2–0.7 m/s at
the failure to ~0.2 m/s on average in the next 5 s. On the benchmark, holding beat extrapolating the last
velocity. For handheld data the smoother's own prior-conditional forecast is used.

**7. What if the organizers' degradation differs from the script?**
Most of the system measures rather than assumes: noise levels per second, severity from packet loss,
rest from data, platform from vibration. We tested both ways the script can be applied to EuRoC files
(columns as-is, and reordered) and three seeds. If the degradation were very different (for example long
dropouts), the smoother still bridges gaps from both sides; we did not tune for that.

**8. How did you validate without the organizers' ground truth?**
We downloaded the real public IMU and ground truth (EuRoC, TUM-VI), degraded them with the unmodified
official script, and split by flight: parameters are chosen on TRAIN flights and reported on TEST flights.
Our degradation-label tool reproduces the official output bit-for-bit, which lets us measure, for
example, spike-detector precision and recall exactly.

**9. Did you use the public ground truth of the evaluation flight?**
No. The estimator never reads ground truth; only the scorer and offline oracle experiments do. The script's
fixed seed would make it possible to fingerprint a public flight or regenerate the noise and subtract
it. We consider that cheating and did neither. Our parameters are a dozen global numbers per platform class.

**10. How did you use AI?**
Claude was the coding and research assistant. Decisions were made by measurement: every change has a
hypothesis, a metric and a before/after in EXPERIMENTS.md, and several ideas were rejected because they did
not help (a body-frame accelerometer-bias model, a Gauss–Markov drag-error state, scenario-specific bias
models).

**11. What fails first under worse jamming?**
The slow error components: accelerometer bias and scale drift late in the flight make the end of the
trajectory, and therefore the blind window, the weakest part. Spikes, frozen samples, duplicates and
short dropouts are handled; very long dropouts would rely entirely on the motion prior.

**12. Why pure Python without numpy?**
The FAQ requires Python, and peak memory is 20 % of the score. Importing numpy alone costs ~7.4 MB of
traced heap. Our estimator's data structures peak at about 2 MB, and runtime is about 5–6 ms per second of IMU
against a 20 ms budget.

**13. Why do you report ~2 MB and not the process memory?**
The FAQ says memory is measured at the heap level (data structures, buffers, estimation logic), not the
interpreter's RSS. We report the estimator's traced heap and, separately, the heap including Python's own
compilation of `main.py`, which is a fixed cost of any Python script.

**14. What are the limits of your approach?**
(a) Slow motion on handheld data is unobservable; we fall back to a learned motion prior. (b) The drag
coefficient is learned for the EuRoC hexacopter; another airframe would need re-fitting. (c) Heading is
only weakly observable without rest periods. (d) The blind 5 s inherits all the error at the moment of
failure.

**15. Exactly one pose per IMU frame: how do you guarantee it?**
The output is written by re-streaming the input rows, one line per row, including duplicates and dead
rows, with timestamps converted by integer arithmetic. If anything fails, a stand-still trajectory with
the same rows is written. 18 malformed-input variants are tested (`tests/test_contract.py`).
